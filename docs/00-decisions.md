| D40 | 2026-10-30 | Smooth scrubbing with three sequences | **High-density laptops get the 1920×1080 frames**; the 2560×1440 frames are only for screens wider than 1920 CSS pixels (narrows D32). Each sequence has three states: **playing** (on screen), **ready** (within 1.5 screens: full window, one decode at a time) and **standby** (further: 8 frames kept, the rest freed). The next sequence stays invisible until it pins through a scroll-driven animation (`ViewTimeline`), which runs inside the browser's own scrolling; the script toggle is only a fallback for browsers without it. | Owner reported delay in all three scenes and a frame of the next scene flashing below the hero on a fast scroll back up. Share of frames trailing the scroll by more than 3, fast scroll at 2×, before → after: hero 41% → 7–9%, connector 48% → 3–13%, glass 25% → 12–16%. At 1× a gentle scroll was already smooth in the connector and glass, and the hero still trails by up to ~8 frames on the test container (no GPU, 4 cores; a laptop decodes several times faster). Decoded-frame memory drops from up to ~750 MB (three sequences × ~250 MB) to about 400 MB once a sequence is left behind. The flash came from the script that hid the next stage running a frame after the scroll; the scroll-driven animation can't lag. Checked on a fast scroll back up through both handoffs: the next sequence never shows before its turn, and the handoff frames still match (3.3 and 2.8 of 255). |
